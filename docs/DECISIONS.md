## Money visibility (2026-10-23, provisional, to be confirmed by the owner on 2026-10-24)

It is enforced in the database (RLS policies and column grants keyed on the request role, D76), not only in the UI; every money table and view has a test proving the roles below get zero rows.

Money views are audited by the app (a database cannot log plain reads): every money read goes through one service that records the view event first.

    - **plant**: stock, deliveries in m³ and recettes only; no price, no money. Unchanged.
    - **partner**: no money access for now, until the owner decides. Keeps operational (non-money) rights: trucks, zones, components, recettes, concrete types, clients and suppliers without balances, reconciliation.

## Review of CP-L4 and CP-OPS-1-3 (2026-10-25)
83. 🟡 (provisional, to confirm with the owner) **Delivery note.** The plant may see and print the client's name and the site on the BL, because the client signs it. No contact, no price, no total, no payment. This changes 81 for the plant on this one document: the plant still reads no client table and no money (`delivery_note()` returns names, places and quantities only). Supersedes the "client code only" line of 81 for the BL.
84. ✅ (review of CP-L4, point 1) **No office door to a price.** An office-context call never receives a price, a total, a client or a load: invoices, orders and quotes are created entirely inside the database (`create_invoice`, `create_order`, `create_quote`) and return the document code only; office has no INSERT policy on the document tables; `resolve_selling_price` answers the owner and the system; the price checks of the guard triggers run after row-level security. `create_invoice` audits every failure and locks an actor out after ten in ten minutes.
85. 🟡 **Payment state of an invoice** is derived, never stored: the covered plan items of the order pay its live invoices, oldest first; paid, partially paid or unpaid. Owner and support only (shown on invoices, statements and the dashboard).
86. 🟡 **Partial receipts.** A purchase can be received in several parts until each line reaches its ordered quantity, each part adding its stock. More than ordered is an over-receipt: only the owner records it, with a confirmation, and it is flagged on the receipt.
87. 🟡 **Loads above the order volume** are allowed (the plant is never blocked), warned about, recorded by the database as an excess and confirmed by the owner (step-up); billed at the order's price like any load. Tolerance parameter `load_excess_tolerance_bp` (500 = 5 % of the order, default 🟡) tells a minor excess from a major one.
88. ✅ **One threshold source.** `stock_threshold` (versioned, audited, owner and partner write) is the only low-stock threshold; the old `component.low_stock_threshold` values were copied into it and the column dropped.
89. 🟡 **Rules engine defaults** (all parameters, ask before changing): 14 working days, safety threshold 0, due alerts 2 days ahead, legal reminders 7 days, bounce reminders at day 20 and 27, purchase lead 2 days, LCN discount 8 % a year, postponement 4 working days, advance 30 % of the order, split 50 %. Overdue receivables are not in the forecast (not counted until paid); an LCN is discounted, a cheque deposited early (D29).
90. 🟡 **Confirming an option** (`cash.decide`, step-up `cash.apply_option`) records a decision with the figures the engine computed; only the early deposit moves anything (the ledger's own deposit, same transaction). Discount, postponement, advance, split and purchase are decisions with a draft message the owner sends himself. The nightly recompute is not wired: the system role has no read path to money tables.

## Integration wave (2026-10-26, from CP-INTEGRATION; all provisional, Ali until the owner confirms)
97. 🟡 **Delivered.** A load is delivered when its rotation records `unloading_finished` (a database trigger writes `delivery_delivered` with the step's own instant), or, for a load made outside any rotation, when the plant, owner or partner marks it (the clock's instant). A load that belongs to a rotation can only be delivered by its tracking. One row per load, insert-only.
98. 🟡 **What counts as delivered quantity.** The view `delivered_load` (caller's own rights) keeps delivered loads that are not cancelled and not wholly lost: a load whose whole volume was refused or brought back (O14) never reached the client; a partial loss still counts, and stays billed until an avoir (93). An order's delivered quantity is the sum of this view. The sales analytics (O15) still count loaded m³ per day; they are not changed here.
99. 🟡 **Stored documents (BL scans, supplier invoice photos).** Images (JPEG, PNG, WebP) and PDF only, by declared type and by magic bytes, with a size limit (parameter `upload_max_bytes`). No image library: JPEG, PNG and WebP are walked and only an allow-list is copied, so GPS, camera, dates, thumbnails and text chunks go; the JPEG's orientation alone is kept, rebuilt as a minimal EXIF, so a phone photo still shows upright. A PDF is stored as received, except that one carrying active names (`/JavaScript`, `/JS`, `/Launch`, `/OpenAction`, `/EmbeddedFile`, `/RichMedia`, `/XFA`, `/Encrypt`, also written with `#` escapes) or no closing marker is refused; a PDF's own metadata is not stripped, and actions hidden in compressed object streams are not seen, so a PDF is only ever downloaded (never shown inline) with `nosniff`. The bytes are sealed with AES-256-GCM under `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`, bound to the file id, in `FILE_STORE_DIR` (default `./file-store`, outside any public path, not in git), one file per id with owner-only permissions; `crypto:rotate` re-seals them. The database keeps who, what and when; a route re-checks the caller's permission and RLS on the owning document before it decrypts, and audits every upload and view.
100. 🟡 **Signed BL and its scan.** The return of the signed BL is recorded once (as before); a scan (photo or PDF) is optional, one per BL, added with the return or later, never before the return and never on a cancelled load; a scan is not replaced (a wrong one is a question for the owner). Plant, office, owner and partner add it; every role that reads loads opens it, support included; each opening is audited (`view` on `file:bl_scan:<BL>`). A refused file records nothing, not even the return. The scan does not gate invoicing.
101. 🟡 **The invoicing queue.** "Delivered, not invoiced" lists the delivered loads (D98) that no live invoice carries, per client. One reviewed SECURITY DEFINER function (`invoice_queue()`) answers owner, support and office, because the client of a load is on its order, which office cannot read; it returns client, BL, dates, product and m³ and nothing else. The amounts (the load at its order's price, one rounding per load, VAT on the HT per rate) are computed by the service for the owner and support only; the answer for office has no money key. Partner and plant have no queue.
102. 🟡 **Invoice from the selected BLs.** One invoice bills the delivered BLs (D98) of one client, across several orders, at one VAT rate; `create_invoice_from_deliveries` is the one door (office sends BL ids and gets the number back, as before; the order-code door now invoices the order's delivered loads through it). The stored lines stay one per load, each rounded once (so the guards, the credit note and "a BL is invoiced once" keep their shape); the document groups them by product and unit price, the quantity summed, the BL numbers listed, the amount of a group being the sum of its stored lines (the same product at two prices is two lines; a group may differ by a centime from quantity times price when a price has odd centimes). The issue deadline (law 69-21) is the end of the month of the earliest delivery billed, stricter than before. Advances the client paid on the orders (plan items of method `advance`) are applied once as a snapshot (`invoice_advance`, per order, up to the share the invoice bills; freed if the invoice is cancelled by a credit note): "Acompte reçu" and "Net à payer" on the document; the invoice total never changes. `invoice.order_id` is empty on new invoices; the orders of an invoice are read from its lines. The payment state of an invoice stays derived (D85) and is now computed by the database (`invoice_payment_state()`, owner and support): what the client paid on each order covers the live invoices that bill it, oldest due date first (then invoice date, number), an invoice's share of an order being its lines of that order with VAT. A load of a past day in the pilot data is delivered by the scenario; the invoice line guard refuses a load that is not delivered.
103. 🟡 **A payment spread over the client's invoices.** A payment entered for a client with no order named (the form's default) is spread by the database (`allocate_to_client`, office blind: a count comes back, no amount) over the client's open invoices, oldest due date first (then invoice date, number), each through the plan items of the orders it bills, oldest due first; what is left covers the client's other open plan items (an advance of an order not invoiced yet); the rest stays on account. The money stays on the plan items (D85); each allocation row carries the invoice it was made for (`allocation.invoice_id`), and the state of an invoice (`invoice_payment_state()`) is its tagged money plus its share of what was paid on the order untagged, oldest due first. The owner directs (part of) a payment to one invoice with `allocate_to_invoice`, a reason of 3 to 500 characters required, kept on the rows (`override_reason`) and so in the audit log; nobody else can; the invoice must be open, of that client. A bounce or an undo reverses the rows with the same tag, which reopens the invoices; the 30-day window of law 71.24 starts as before. Without the flag (the order-code and manual ways, the services' default) nothing is spread.
104. 🟡 **The avoir for refused or returned concrete** (answer of 2026-10-26 to D93: the concrete stays billed until an avoir). A separate document (`avoir`, AV-YYYY-nnnnn from its own gapless counter), owner only, with a fresh second factor (sensitive action `ledger.credit_note`), written only by `issue_avoir` (SECURITY DEFINER): it references the invoice that bills the load, the BL and the loss record, one per loss record, and never edits the invoice. It credits the lost litres at the invoice line's price and VAT (one rounding per figure). The credit is applied onto the plan items of the BL's order, oldest due first, as allocation rows with an avoir instead of an instrument (tagged with the invoice), so the order's open amount, the invoice's state, the deadlines and the forecast all follow; a credit beyond what is open stays an unapplied credit in the client's favour (shown as applied 0 on the avoir). The client statement carries it as a counted commercial line (negative). It does not move cash: a refund is a separate decision. The avoir cannot be undone from the app (a mistaken avoir is the owner's question). Full cancellation of an invoice stays the existing credit note (FA number).
105. 🟡 **The supplier invoice is its own document.** Linked to one purchase order (the `purchase` of before is the order) and to the receipts it covers, with the lines as the supplier wrote them and our code FF-YYYY-nnnnn. Entered through `create_supplier_invoice` (owner, or office blind: our code comes back and nothing else); the database links each line to the order's line of the same component, computes the totals (quantity in the unit of the order line, one rounding per line and for the VAT, at the order's VAT rate) and refuses, with one audited answer, an unknown order, a bad date (not in the future, due date at most 120 days after, law 69-21) or a supplier number already entered for that supplier (case-insensitive; a rejected invoice frees its number). It covers the receipts named, else every receipt of the order no live invoice covers yet, so two invoices of one order take the receipts in turn. Money: owner and support read; nobody inserts directly. The match, the approval and the payable follow (I2.2).
106. 🟡 **Three-way match and the payable of a purchase.** A supplier invoice is compared line by line with its order and the receipts it covers: the quantity billed against what was received through those receipts (a service line, which is not received, is skipped) and, over every live invoice of the order line, against what was ordered; the unit price against the order's; a line the order does not have is a gap too. The tolerances are the parameters `match_qty_tolerance_bp` (100 = 1 %) and `match_price_tolerance_bp` (50 = 0,5 %), read by the database, which falls back to those defaults when the owner has set nothing. A gap blocks "approve for payment" until the owner accepts it with a reason (3 to 500 characters), after a fresh second factor (`purchases.approve_invoice`); the acceptance is an event row (reason, actor, time) and so in the audit log. The owner may also reject an invoice not yet approved (its supplier number is free again and its receipts too). **The payable comes from the approved invoice**: approval writes the payment plan items of that invoice on its order (one transfer on the invoice's due date, or the plan the owner gives, adding up to the invoice total, due at most 120 days after the invoice), and the order made the new way (`purchase.payable_source = 'invoice'`, the default) carries no plan and no invoice number of its own; orders made before keep theirs (`'order'`) and cannot be approved through an invoice. An order cannot be cancelled while an invoice not rejected stands on it. The supplier statement counts the approved invoice (what is owed) and shows the order as a commitment that is not counted; the purchases journal of the accountant lists orders that carry their own payable and approved invoices. A supplier payment names the invoice by its FF code (or the order by its ACH code for the older way) and covers the oldest open items.
107. 🟡 **The photo of a supplier invoice, and the AI reading of it.** The photo (or PDF, up to five documents per invoice) is stored as an attachment of the invoice with the rules of D99: type and magic bytes, metadata stripped, sealed at rest, opened only by an authorized route (owner and support) that re-checks RLS, every view audited. A refused file at entry leaves no invoice behind. **AI extraction is an interface only**: a draft the person checks in the normal form (never an amount computed by a model), behind the flag `AI_EXTRACTION_ENABLED`, which is OFF by default and is refused at boot together with `REAL_DATA=true` or in production until the CNDP has authorized the transfer (open question 4); with the flag off the door answers `DISABLED` before it touches the file, with the flag on and no provider wired (this release) it answers `NOT_AVAILABLE`; neither makes any network call, a test spies on every way out. 108. 🟡 **The order chain.** One row per order (newest delivery date first, 300 at most) with six stages: ordered, planned (live rotations), loaded (net of cancellations), delivered (D98), invoiced (lines of live invoices, litres) and paid (the payment state of the order: unpaid, partial, paid). Litres and states only, never an amount. Every role of the company reads it through one SECURITY DEFINER function (`order_chain()`), because office cannot read orders and the plant reads only a view of operational fields; the paid stage is NULL, shown as a dash, for office, plant and partner, and the owner's and support's reads of it are the money views they already are (support's is audited). The client appears by its code only; the order's number is a link only for a role that can open the order. The first stage not complete is shown as what the order waits for.
109. 🟡 **Related documents.** Every document page (BL, order, quote, invoice, purchase order, supplier invoice) lists its neighbours by number: BL to order, invoices, avoirs and the payments covering its order; order to BLs, invoices, avoirs, payments and the quote it came from; invoice to its orders and BLs, avoirs and payments; purchase order to receipts, supplier invoices and payments; supplier invoice to its order, receipts and payments. The panel is not printed. Avoirs and payments link to their list pages (they have no page of their own); receipts are shown by their date. Nothing in the panel carries an amount.

116. 🟡 **Tools: allow-lists, units, and the numbers rule.** Each tool returns what its output schema names (per role) and nothing else; a role without money gets no money key, not even a null. Quantities are converted by code (m³; tonnes and litres for stock) so that the model never converts and no quantity is a run of six digits (a run of six digits in any payload is a leak by rule). The post-check accepts a number only if a tool returned it this turn; dates, times and months as the data wrote them; codes and names that carry digits (T1, B25) as the data or the question wrote them. The check is on the number, not on the unit: a quantity of the right value in the wrong unit would pass (provisional).
117. 🟡 **Drafts.** The model writes codes, enumerations and bounded numbers; an amount or a cheque number is a reference (`{{AMT_n}}`, `{{NUM_n}}`) to what the person typed, resolved by code, so no figure of the model's own reaches a service. A price is the person's, else the supplier price in force for the roles that read purchase prices; office, who cannot read prices, must type it (so the "price in force" of the spec is the owner's path). Confirmation is one atomic claim (pending, confirming, confirmed or failed), only by its owner, once, within 24 hours, with the human as actor inside `asAiConfirmation` so the audit says `ai_confirmed` and the proposal id; a draft whose data moved fails closed. A draft names a client by code; a site by `SITE-<client>-<n>`, but the pre-pass does not yet map site names to those codes (limit).
118. 🟡 **Names, numbers and the leak scan.** Names of clients and suppliers become codes before the model and come back as the names the person's own screens show; a role that reads no client (the plant) has no names to mask, and what it types is caught by the scan. The scan covers every name, contact, driver, user, plate, site, bank, ICE, instrument number and sealed phone or address (one reviewed definer function for the system role, migration 0077, cached 30 s); it reports kinds, never values; a hit stops the turn before any provider call and is recorded `blocked_leak`; if the index cannot be built nothing is sent. A name that is also a product label is a false positive that fails closed.
119. 🟡 **Limits, switches, budget, retention.** Kill switches (global, per role), 20 questions in 10 minutes and 200 a day per user (stricter-only), and a monthly budget (USD 20) are parameters; the owner sees a warning in the panel from 80 %, there is no stored alert yet; cost uses provisional per-token constants (3 and 15 micro-dollars) until the provider's invoice and the evaluation (OQ5). Payloads (question, answer, draft parameters) are sealed with `FIELD_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`, rotated by `crypto:rotate`, and emptied after 90 days by `assistant:purge` (not scheduled: the jobs are stubs). Each person reads their own rows only, the owner included.
120. 🟡 **The scripted provider and its evaluation set.** The Fake provider routes by keywords (French, Arabic, Darija in Arabic or Latin letters) to the tool among those offered and writes its answer from the tool results with their placeholders, so it passes the same checks as a model. The 20 questions per role are ours, not the owner's real phrasings. "The next load with its mix" is the concrete type of the load: there is no recette tool in v1. The endpoints are route handlers (`/api/assistant/*`) that answer 404 with the flag off, accept a write only from the application's own origin, and are tested without a server (`endpoints.ts`); the e2e runs a test server with the flag on and a second one with it off.
